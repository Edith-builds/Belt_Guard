What am I looking at?
  • Carry belt (top, troughed): moves from the tail pulley (loading end, low) to the head pulley (discharge end, high). It's shaped into a shallow U by 3-roll troughing idlers so loose ore rides in the middle without spilling.
  • Return belt (bottom, flat): the empty strand running back down to the tail, riding on widely-spaced flat return idlers.
  • Head pulley: larger, rubber-lagged, driven by the motor/gearbox shown as the housing at the discharge end.
  • Tail pulley: at the loading end, backed by a take-up counterweight that keeps belt tension constant.
  • Trestle: raked steel legs + cross-bracing carrying the whole gallery, spaced along the incline.
  • Walkway & handrail: continuous inspection access down the side(s) of the gallery, posts kept vertical (as gravity, not the incline, dictates rail geometry).
  • Ore load: chunks piled according to a natural angle-of-repose profile — highest at the trough centre, tapering toward the edges.
Geometry ranges (trough angle 20/35/45°, carry spacing ~1.0–1.5 m, return spacing ~2.5–3.5 m, incline capped at 18°) follow standard CEMA-style troughed-belt conveyor practice for bulk ore handling.
